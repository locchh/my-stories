Lan was **reluctant** to listen to her own voice. Whenever she recorded herself speaking English, she heard every pause and every unclear ending. The recording made her uncomfortable, so she usually deleted it after a few seconds.

One morning, she tried a different approach. She stood in front of a mirror, opened her mouth wider than usual, and read a page aloud. She decided to **exaggerate** every movement of her lips and tongue. At first, the exercise looked ridiculous, but that was exactly why it worked.

## A small daily practice

For five minutes each day, Lan focused on **articulation** instead of speed. She paid attention to final sounds and gave every important word enough space. A week later, her speech sounded more **crisp**, and she no longer rushed through difficult sentences.

The change was not dramatic, but it was visible. That small sign of progress gave her the confidence to continue. She kept each recording instead of deleting it, because every imperfect attempt had become evidence that she was moving forward.

> Confidence did not arrive before practice. It grew from practice.
