After watching a short documentary, Mai wrote twelve new words in a notebook. She could recognise each one, but when she tried to speak, none of them came to mind. Reading the list again did not help her **retain** them.

Instead of memorising the words alone, Mai wrote a short story about the people in the documentary. This gave every word a **context**: a place, a feeling, and a reason to exist. The next time she saw one of those words, she remembered the scene around it.

## From recognition to recall

Mai then covered the meanings and tested herself. This **deliberate** effort felt slower than rereading, but it exposed the words she did not truly know. Each difficult **encounter** became a useful signal rather than a failure.

Finally, she read her story aloud and recorded it. She repeated it until the sentences flowed without the notebook. Her **fluency** did not come from learning hundreds of new words. It came from using a few valuable words again and again.

The words stayed because they were no longer items on a list. They had become part of a story she understood.
