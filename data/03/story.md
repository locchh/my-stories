When Daniel moved into the apartment upstairs, he rarely greeted anyone. He was quiet in the lift and **reserved** at neighbourhood gatherings. Linh quickly decided that her new neighbour was unfriendly.

Her **prejudice** grew from small moments. When Daniel looked away, she assumed he was rude. When he left a party early, she took it as proof that he disliked everyone there. She never considered that she might **misinterpret** his silence.

## The parcel at the door

One rainy evening, Linh found a parcel outside her apartment. Daniel had carried it upstairs before the rain could damage it. He had also left a short, **gracious** note explaining what had happened.

The next morning, Linh thanked him. During their conversation, she learned that Daniel was still building confidence in Vietnamese and often worried about saying the wrong thing. His silence was not arrogance; it was caution.

That brief conversation turned a stranger into an **acquaintance**. Linh realised that a first impression may feel complete even when it is built from missing information. From then on, she replaced quick conclusions with one simple question: “What else could this mean?”
